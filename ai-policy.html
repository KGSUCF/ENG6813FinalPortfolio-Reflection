AI as a tool, not an authority

The policy is course-wide, non-punitive, and student-facing. It assumes students will use AI, and it tells them exactly what responsible use looks like.

The policy, in full

The authoritative text of the course-wide AI policy.

AI may be used in this course as a tool for brainstorming, organization, revision, accessibility, and analysis, but it may not replace your own close reading, evidence-checking, or interpretation. You are responsible for every assignment you submit and should be able to explain your choices, sources, tool use, evidence, and conclusions.

Any AI use must be documented. When you use AI, briefly identify the tool, what you asked it to do, what it helped with, what you checked, and what you changed or rejected. Undisclosed AI use, unverified AI claims, or AI-generated work submitted as your own analysis will not meet course requirements.

You are also responsible for copyright and attribution. Use public-domain, openly licensed, government-produced, instructor-approved, or otherwise legally usable materials for course projects. Do not upload copyrighted, private, or proprietary materials into AI tools unless you have permission or the assignment specifically allows it. Any AI-generated or AI-assisted content must be clearly identified.

Paid AI subscriptions are not required. Assignments can be completed with free or institutionally available tools, and alternatives will be available when access, disability, privacy, language, or technology limitations affect a student’s ability to use a particular tool.

Access is part of the policy, not a footnote

No paid AI subscription is required to succeed in this course. When access, disability, privacy, language, or technology limitations affect a student’s ability to use a particular tool, an alternative is provided. A policy that assumes every student has the same tools, the same bandwidth, and the same comfort uploading their work to a third party is not a policy about responsible AI use — it is a filter.

Where students practice it

Week 11 introduces chatbots as interpretive assistants and asks for a comparison memo: what did I see, what did AI see, what did AI miss, flatten, or overstate? Week 12 turns that into a documented AI-use note. By the signature assignment, documentation is routine rather than a disclosure ritual.
