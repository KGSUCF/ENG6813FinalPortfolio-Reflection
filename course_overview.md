# Course Overview

Students need more than traditional “media literacy” in the narrow sense. They need practical, humanistic tools for reading persuasive messages, evaluating evidence, recognizing misleading uses of images and numbers, and understanding the role of technology in shaping belief.

**Media Literacy in the Humanities** is an introductory course about meaning-making, persuasion, misinformation, and judgment in an AI-mediated world. Students will examine the stories, emotions, platforms, habits, and assumptions that influence what people notice, trust, question, and share. They will also consider how their own attention, biases, identities, and interpretive habits affect what they believe.

The course does not assume technical expertise or require prerequisites. Instead, it invites students to slow down, ask better questions, and practice responsible interpretation. Media literacy is treated not as a specialized technical skill, but as part of modern humanistic education: learning to read, question, interpret, and act responsibly in a world full of persuasive messages.
