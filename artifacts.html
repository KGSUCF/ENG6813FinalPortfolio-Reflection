Teaching Artifacts

Materials built for this course

Instructor-made teaching tools, each designed to make one specific move visible to students. These are not equally weighted — three carry the course.

Week 4 Instructor-created game

Point Taken

A fallacy-identification game used in the Week 4 workshop on fallacies, omissions, weak reasoning, and fair persuasion. Students play it before attempting the Rhetorical Design Lab, where they build a persuasive message containing both sound and flawed reasoning.

Recognizing a fallacy in someone else’s argument is much easier than catching it in your own. Playing first makes the second task possible.

Framing note: students play Point Taken. They are not expected to build an agentic game like it — no coding is required anywhere in this course.

Week 8 Data rhetoric

1% Tax Visualization

The primary data-rhetoric example in the course, used in the Week 8 workshop on images, numbers, graphs, and selective evidence.

The same dataset supports different persuasive claims depending on what is emphasized, omitted, or visually framed. Students see that a chart can be accurate and misleading at the same time — which is harder to accept, and more useful, than catching an outright lie.

Week 10 Digital humanities

Lincoln Speeches Explorer

A model for the digital humanities unit: an instructor-built tool showing how AI-assisted code can help explore a large public-domain corpus.

Digital tools extend humanities interpretation by increasing scale, but they do not replace human judgment. The explorer is shown alongside Voyant work so students can ask what pattern-reading reveals — and what it flattens.

The corpus is public domain, which is itself part of the lesson: what you are permitted to analyze shapes what you can study.

AI Policy Interactive explainer

AI Policy Cartoon

An illustrated, interactive explanation of the course AI policy. Five panels — no AI at all, copy and paste, ghost citations, the copyright shortcut, and document and check — each paired with the policy line it illustrates.

Course policies can be clear, non-punitive, and student-facing. This one is shown in full on the AI Policy page rather than filed in this gallery.
