# Syllabus Summary

## Course title and number

**ENG 2011: Media Literacy in the Humanities**

## Delivery model

This course is imagined as a Fall 2026, in-person, full-semester UCF course meeting Tuesday/Thursday. Tuesday meetings introduce concepts, readings, and guided analysis. Thursday meetings function as active-learning workshops where students practice course methods collaboratively before completing individual submissions.

Zoom recordings and Canvas discussion support access, review, and peer engagement, but the course is designed as an in-person class.

## Course units

### Unit 1: Rhetoric and Persuasion
The first unit introduces rhetoric and persuasion, including emotional appeals, framing, authority, repetition, audience, fallacies, omissions, weak reasoning, and fair persuasion.

### Unit 2: Human Vulnerability to Misinformation
The second unit examines cognitive bias, identity, outrage, first reactions, belonging, and the desire for simple stories.

### Unit 3: Technology-Based Persuasion
The third unit explores social media platforms, algorithms, advertising systems, search engines, generative AI, and digital humanities tools.

### Unit 4: Better Learning and Better Judgment
The final unit focuses on source evaluation, lateral reading, data context, fact-checking habits, ethical AI use, reflective skepticism, and public-facing communication.

## Materials and tools

No paid AI subscription is required. Students will use freely available or institutionally available tools, which may include Canvas, web browsers, library resources, Voyant or comparable text-analysis tools, search engines, reverse image/source tracing tools, and a chatbot such as Claude or another approved tool.

All substantial course projects must use public-domain, openly licensed, government-produced, instructor-approved, or otherwise legally usable materials.
