Teaching Statement

Fail safely, revise with evidence

The pedagogy underneath the course design.

I believe students learn more deeply when they are given structured chances to attempt difficult thinking, fail safely, and revise with evidence. That belief drives the design of Media Literacy in the Humanities, which pairs traditional humanities methods with digital and AI-assisted inquiry.

Students move from close, manual interpretation of short texts to analysis of larger collections of texts and information environments that exceed what any one reader could examine alone. At each stage, they compare their own interpretations against machine-generated or tool-assisted results. This reinforces a central claim of the course: computational methods shape interpretation, but they do not determine it.

The course culminates in a signature assignment built with the end goal in mind from day one. Students investigate a real persuasive message or information environment and produce a public-facing guide explaining how it works on its audience: what it emphasizes, what it leaves out, what it makes easy to believe, and what it makes harder to question. They use close reading, evidence-checking, and at least one AI tool, and they must document their process, justify their choices, and reflect on where the tool helped and where it misled them.

My approach to AI follows the same scaffolding logic as the rest of the course. Generative AI is introduced only after students have built independent analytical skills. From there, students may use it for brainstorming, organization, accessibility, and analysis, but never as a substitute for their own interpretation and never to supply a factual claim that is not verified against the underlying text or data.

As an educator, I return to one belief across all of this: students learn best when they can fail safely, revise with evidence, and explain not only what they produced but how their tools and methods shaped it. My goal is to cultivate interpretive responsibility: students who can use generative AI and computational methods to extend their work while keeping their own judgment intact.
