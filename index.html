Media Literacy in the Humanities

Reading persuasion carefully — and keeping your own judgment intact as the tools get better at thinking for you.

Students need more than “media literacy” in the narrow sense. They need practical, humanistic tools for reading persuasive messages, evaluating evidence, recognizing misleading uses of images and numbers, and understanding how technology shapes belief.

This is an introductory course about meaning-making, persuasion, misinformation, and judgment in an AI-mediated world — and about how our own attention, biases, and habits decide what we notice, trust, question, and share.

The course is built as an arc

Nothing is introduced before students can evaluate it. Skills accumulate in a deliberate order, and each stage gives them the judgment they need to question the next one.

  1. Close reading first

    Weeks 1–8. Students slow down and read persuasive messages by hand: audience, purpose, framing, emotional appeals, fallacies, missing context.

  2. Then tools

    Weeks 9–10. Platforms, algorithms, and text-analysis tools enter only once students have something to compare machine output against.

  3. AI as a checked assistant

    Weeks 11–12. Generative AI arrives last, and always alongside verification: what did it see, miss, flatten, or overstate?

  4. Public-facing communication

    Weeks 13–14 and the final. Students turn analysis into a public guide that helps other people read more carefully.

What this course believes

Fail safely, revise with evidence

Students learn more deeply when they are given structured chances to attempt difficult thinking, fail safely, and revise with evidence — not when they are asked to be right the first time.

Tools shape interpretation, they don't determine it

At every stage students compare their own reading against tool-assisted results. Computational methods extend interpretation; they do not settle it.

Interpretive responsibility

The goal is students who can explain not only what they produced, but how their tools and methods shaped it.
