Learning Objectives

What students will be able to do

Six outcomes, sequenced so that each one supplies the judgment the next one depends on.

Objective 1
Remember

Recognize manipulation

Students will identify common techniques used to influence belief and behavior, including emotional appeals, misleading images, selective data, repetition, false authority, and outrage.

Objective 2
UnderstandAnalyze

Read persuasive messages closely

Students will analyze how language, images, numbers, layout, genre, and platform design shape meaning and audience response.

Objective 3
AnalyzeEvaluate

Question evidence

Students will evaluate sources by asking what is known, what is missing, what is being assumed, who benefits, and what additional context is needed.

Objective 4
EvaluateUnderstand

Reflect on their own role as interpreters

Students will explain how bias, emotion, identity, convenience, and social pressure can affect what they believe and share.

Objective 5
ApplyEvaluate

Use AI as a tool, not an authority

Students will use AI tools to assist with inquiry while checking their outputs against credible sources and human judgment.

Objective 6
CreateApply

Communicate responsible knowledge

Students will create a clear, accessible project that teaches others how to interpret information more carefully and act more responsibly in digital life.

Designed backward from the final project

Every objective is assessed in the signature assignment. Students choose a real persuasive message, read it closely, check its evidence, document their tool use, reflect on their own interpretation, and write a public-facing guide — one project that exercises all six outcomes at once.

Where the objectives sit in Bloom’s taxonomy

Mapping each objective against cognitive levels is how the course design was checked for a specific failure: an introductory course that never asks students to do more than recall and recognize.
